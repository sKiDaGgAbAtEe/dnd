Cold Open

Three members down. Fable gone. Accel Signal gone. Raven still absent. What remains is Evo with no spell slots, Izabella who cannot remember where she wrote her spells down, Sekhmet who has been raring to go since before anyone else finished their tea, and Malc Grann, who is back from heating up corn and ready to whip something.

Behind the door that already has a 5mm hole in it — Evo’s hole, made last session when he was killing goblins through it — something is waiting. The party has been in there long enough to wonder if it knows they’re coming.

I ain’t got no spells, Lieutenant Dan.

✶
I — The Diplomatic Incident

Sekhmet goes first. Not because he was sent — nobody sent him. He simply walked through the door while everyone was still arguing about whether to do it, found a hallway with torchlight and blood on the far wall, and crept far enough to see what was in the next room: a Goblin King, awake, mounted on a creature large enough to be described as “that big motherfucker.” He rolled an 18 on stealth and an 18 on investigation and came back to report with the energy of someone who has just confirmed something interesting rather than terrifying.

The creature is large. The goblin on top of it has a mace. He is not asleep. He is aware, at minimum, that something has been moving in the tunnels outside his room. Sekhmet was close enough to see his face and remained unseen.

The party convenes on the question of language. Neither Evo nor Malc Grann speak Goblin. Malc Grann speaks Gnomish and can communicate with lizards, a fact that is noted and set aside. Someone floats the idea of a diplomatic approach. Sekhmet, who at this point has the most health and the strongest melee damage, decides this means him.

Sekhmet
“Okay — I’m a social barbarian.”

He walks over. He rolls a 17 on persuasion. The Goblin King looks down at him, grunts in a language no one present understands, and points toward the door they came from. The meaning is clear. Sekhmet requests clarification on which door, gets no satisfying answer, and tries again. His luck runs out. The goblin slams his mace on the ground and points again, more firmly. The diplomatic window is closing.

Izabella steps forward. She wants to try. Everyone agrees this is a reasonable idea because Evo has a +5 to Persuasion and they should have sent him, but they didn’t, and now Izabella is already moving across the room toward the Goblin King with purpose.

Izabella
“Sir — if you could just let us squeeze through your thighs, we will be able to conduct our business and leave.”

She rolls a nat 1. The Goblin King gets mad. Evo, who has been watching this from across the room, jumps in front of her and attempts to apologize with a flat charisma check. He rolls a 6. The goblin rolls a nat 20 and hits Evo for 8 damage.

Everyone is now in combat.

✶ ✶ ✶
II — The King Falls

The first thing the Goblin King does is hit Evo with his mace for 8 damage. Evo’s response is immediate: Wrath of the Storm, the cleric reaction that fires back when an attacker connects. The goblin makes a Dexterity save, rolls a 17, succeeds, and takes half damage. Evo uses Channel Divinity to push it to maximum: 2d8 lightning pushed to 8, halved to 4. He also uses his turn to smack the goblin with his mace and drink his healing potion, because he is severely injured and has no spell slots and is doing his best.

Izabella, who caused this, attempts Witch Bolt and rolls a 1. She rolls again and gets a 2. She considers calling it a night. The DM suggests changing her dice color. She changes to a different color, rolls a 16 plus her +3 spellcasting modifier for a 19, and hits for 8 damage. A miracle. She annotates this fact to herself.

Sekhmet rages — properly, verbally, with a declaration of intent — and attacks recklessly with his sun disc club. First swing: 14 plus 3 strength, hits. Damage: 7 plus 3, 10 points. Second round: 14 plus 3 again, hits. Damage: 7 plus 3 again, 10 points. The consistency is notable. He deals 10 damage on essentially every swing, with the focus and reliability of a machine.

Malc Grann whips. His first attack lands for 8 damage — max damage on his d4 — which the DM receives with the reverence it deserves. His second attack, a nat 20 on the to-hit, he doubles his dice: 5 plus 7, 12 damage. The goblin is now described as “bloodied.”

The goblin spends most of his turns targeting Evo specifically — missing twice, hitting once more for 6 — and the lizard cleric absorbs it with the dignity of someone who made poor choices and is accepting the consequences. Evo fires his lightning breath weapon in a 30-foot line: the goblin fails his save and takes 7 damage; the beast behind him makes its save and takes 5.

Final round. Izabella switches her dice to a third color and rolls a 16 to hit, a 19 with her modifier. Four damage. The DM asks her to describe the kill.

She pauses. I use Ray of Frost and it hits him in the face and… he dies.

Sekhmet adds the detail she leaves out: his face falls off. The Goblin King shatters into frozen pieces. The creature he was riding is now riderless, blasted by lightning, and very angry.

✶ ✶ ✶
III — Taming the Beast

The creature has been sitting in this room its entire life with a goblin on its back and 5 damage from Evo’s breath weapon. It has opinions. The party, to their credit, immediately decides they want to keep it.

Sekhmet goes first. He walks up to it and hugs it. Animal handling with disadvantage — it has just been blasted and its master is dead — and he rolls a 5, the low of his two dice. He adds his +3 modifier. He has an 8. The creature bear hugs him back for 8 points of damage. The table interprets this as reciprocal affection. It is not clear the table is wrong.

Malc Grann whips it. He announces this is his training method — degradation, correction, reward only for good behavior — and rolls animal handling. Whatever he rolls, it is high enough that the DM runs a taming minigame, tracking something called “affection.” The whip brings affection down. This is noted.

Evo throws it a femur. He rolls an 18 on animal handling. Affection goes up. He marks minus 1 femur in his inventory. He has three left.

Izabella remembers she has Friend of the Forest. She can communicate with beasts in a limited way — they understand her words, she cannot understand them — and she has advantage on persuasion checks to influence them. She rolls a 13 and a 19. The creature follows the trail of bones Malc Grann lays in a path across the room and into the corner, clearing the way to the dark hole behind it.

Izabella
“Hey friend — we’ve got some bones for you to follow. Would you like to come with us?”

It follows the bones. The hole is open.

✶ ✶ ✶
IV — The Source

Evo tries to collect the Goblin King’s body. He fails his strength check — the upper half is frozen solid and fused to the creature’s back — and manages only to rip off the skull. He now has a hobgoblin skull and a goblin skull. He catalogs this.

Izabella jumps in the hole without telling anyone. She can fly; she argues this makes it fine. The rest of the party follows.

Below: a portal, and a ritual pot. This is it — the source of the corruption that has been poisoning the town’s water supply, the reason they went into the sewers, the thing at the end of the quest. It is inert. Whatever was feeding it has stopped, or paused, or is waiting for something. The party has a moment to breathe.

The DM tells them they level up to Level 3.

Izabella cheers. She does not immediately know what Level 3 gives her. Sekhmet needs to choose his subclass. Evo and Malc Grann apparently already know what they want. Izabella is instructed to do her homework before next session, because Level 3 is also when wizards unlock new spells and she has decisions to make.

The session ends there, in the dark below the sewers, beside an inert ritual pot and a portal to somewhere unknown, with a tamed beast eating bones upstairs and a goblin skull in Evo’s pocket.
