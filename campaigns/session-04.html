Cold Open

Something has changed.

They feel it before they see it — the particular silence of a town that went to sleep one size and woke up another. New storefronts overnight. A waterway where there was none. Crops that were bare yesterday are green today, leaning into a sun they weren’t there to greet the morning before.

Izabella, recording again with her characteristic cheerful chaos, asks the obvious question: Is this because we killed the goblins?

Yes, Illy says. Yes, it is.

The world has noticed them. It moved in response.

They don’t have long to marvel. Something steps out of a portal.

✶
I — A Stranger Through the Portal

The elf who stumbles out of the portal is not composed. He’s talking before he’s fully arrived, mid-sentence from whatever plane he just left, muttering about portals happening every damn time. He is tall — six feet, long silver hair, blue-tinged eyes, a bow across his back and strange blue markings framing his face. Dark leather armor, a deep blue cloak, and the overall impression of someone who has been many places and not entirely chosen any of them.

His name, eventually, is Accel Signal. An eladrin elf — a particular kind, he explains, one whose skin should shift with the seasons, though his is permanently pale, locked still by something he waves past without elaborating on. He has been traveling alone through portals for a long time. He has a book he won’t fully explain. And he has an ale.

Accel Signal
“In my travels I’ve seen far and wide — different portals, different planes. And when I see talent, when I see strong potential, I seek it out. I see it in you lot.”

The party receives this with varying degrees of skepticism. Evo notes, helpfully, that making allies from this particular group is a terrible mistake. Sekhmet watches with the quiet evaluation of someone deciding how much trouble the new arrival is going to be. Izabella, ever direct: What’s in it for us?

What’s in it for them is Memory Ale. Accel produces it from his bag with real ceremony — rare across any plane, he says, made to call forward the taste of whatever memory you love most. He pours a cup for each of them. The trick, he explains: focus on the memory as you drink.

Izabella looks at her cup, decides hers smells better, and offers him a taste of her drink instead. Fable observes quietly that one should not take hazing drinks from fey. The fey don’t haze — they give you ADHD. Accel rolls constitution. He gets a 17. The drink goes down smooth. He is delighted. Izabella is annoyed.

Evo
“Before we continue — one of our party members happens to be a god, if you’d believe that.”
Accel Signal
“I’ve met many gods. I speak some of their language, depending on the god.”
Evo
“He’s incredibly powerful. All these buildings you see before us — because of him.”

Malc Grann arrives — slightly late, freshly awake, immediately informed that he built the entire expanded town overnight in his sleep. He examines the nearest wall with his magnifying glass. Every brick is identical. He believes it completely. Accel opens his book, stares at it, nods, and closes it. He is on board.

Izabella asks what they’re actually doing here. Accel has a problem. The well beneath the church — something is wrong with it, poisoning the water and the crops. The guard confirms it. The party has a new quest before they’ve finished breakfast.

They head for the undercroft.

✶ ✶ ✶
II — Into the Undercroft

The church undercroft is dark in the way torchlit tunnels are dark — not uniformly, but in patches, the flames casting hard pools of orange that make the shadows deeper by contrast. There is a debate about who has darkvision. There is a debate about the 2014 versus 2024 rulebook. Evo does not have darkvision and gestures for Malc Grann to climb onto his head. This is now established procedure. The Ratatouille system is operational.

Izabella runs ahead. Evo grabs her. She escapes. She runs ahead again.

Fable rolls a 20 on perception and sees what nobody else does: a goblin, half-emerged from a crack in the wall. The cracks are everywhere, he realizes — perfectly goblin-sized, deliberate, a network threaded through the stone. The tunnels are not accidental. This is infrastructure.

Fable
“The way the torches cast their light obscures the holes from which the goblins seem to be hiding. They might try to ambush us.”

Sekhmet hangs back. The goblin hasn’t spotted the party. It is crouched, half-out, focused on something ahead. Sekhmet does not announce himself.

Sekhmet brings his club down. First blood — instant, clean, silent. 10 points of damage. The party turns to find a body without having heard a fight.

A second goblin backstabs at Evo and misses. Initiative is rolled. Fable fires his crossbow — misses — then passes Accel a Bardic Inspiration and steps back to watch.

Accel Signal raises his longbow, applies Hunter’s Mark, and shoots. Nat 20. Then another nat 20. The table reacts the way tables react to consecutive natural 20s — a particular mixture of awe and offense. We’re starting to see why you needed teammates. You’re not wrong. It’s very cramped in here.

Evo casts Sanctuary on Malc Grann — the gnome on his head glowing faintly with divine protection — then whips a goblin straight in the throat. It dies gurgling.

Izabella lines up Firebolt on the last goblin. Rolls 18 to hit. Rolls 8 damage. She describes it: the fireball going straight into its open mouth, down the throat, cooking from the inside out. The eyeballs follow. The body falls smoking. Does anyone smell that roasting smell? Fable, quietly: I wish I didn’t.

✶ ✶ ✶
III — The God of the Wall

Deeper in, the tunnels open. More goblin-sized cracks. More shadows shifting. Before the party can regroup, a wave pours out of the walls from every direction at once.

Ambush.

Accel goes first. His opening shot is a miss, then he uses the bardic inspiration — still a miss. The goblins, emboldened, fire back. A slingshot hits Accel for 10 points. He removes the projectile immediately. Evo notes you’re not supposed to do that. Accel doesn’t stop.

The goblin mage — of course there is a goblin mage — attempts Firebolt at Evo and misses. Attempts it again, rolls a nat 20, and hits Malc Grann on Evo’s head for 7 damage. Malc Grann does not fall. He barely registers it. The goblins quietly stop shooting at him.

Accel Signal
“You’re a god! I can see why — I really see why now.”
Fable
“Yeah. I wasn’t sure if you were telling the truth before, but now I’m kind of on board.”

Evo commits fully to the bit. He whips the goblin mage across the forehead. It dies. He uses Action Surge. Illy grants him advantage on the next attack, because he was tricked into believing he built the entire town overnight and is now surrounded by enemies — and the faith of an idiot is still faith. The Wall God ascends.

Sekhmet misses twice, then unlocks Rage for the first time. Illy walks him through it mid-combat — roll twice, take the higher. He rolls 11 plus 5. 16 hits. Club down, shield through, goblin into the ground. A second goblin runs past him to reach Izabella. His attack of opportunity catches it mid-stride. It never touched her.

Izabella positions over Malc Grann and unleashes Thunderclap for the first time this campaign. The detonation deafens every living creature on the floor. Two goblins fail their saves — both explode. She says, in the ringing silence: You’re welcome, Malkren. Nobody hears her.

Fable misses twice with his crossbow. Then Accel looks over and says you’ve got this, I believe in you. Fable puts the crossbow away, walks up to the last goblin, weathers its attack of opportunity, and casts Dissonant Whispers directly into its brain. It seizes. It bleeds from the face. It falls. The party quietly recalibrates how gentle the owlman actually is.

Accel works through the far goblins methodically with Hunter’s Mark reapplied and adjusted, killing with the patient efficiency of someone who has done this across many planes. He missed a few shots. He notes this happens sometimes. He is not troubled.

The last goblin falls to Sekhmet, raging, bardic inspiration stacked on top. The door at the end of the hall slides open on its own — previously locked, now not.

✶ ✶ ✶
IV — After the Fight

Everyone is deafened. Fable is relaying messages telepathically. Accel is reading lips and doing reasonably well at it. Evo is looting.

He searches four separate goblins. A nat 20: two femurs, a skull, one rib. A 22: three more ribs. A 9: a hand. A 19: skull, spine, full rib cage. He collects these without comment. No one asks. No one stops him. When Malc Grann wanders over, Evo offers a high five using the retrieved goblin hand. Malc Grann goes in for it.

Accel finds a single poison arrow in the rubble and adds it to inventory.

The unlocked door waits ahead. The source of the poisoned water lies somewhere beyond it. Illy checks in — in real life, everyone is tired. Izabella has been awake since 4 AM. The DM just finished a 12-hour shift. The decision is made to call it and continue as a two-parter.

Nobody died. Izabella is at 7 HP. The god of the wall is untouched. A new ranger has integrated into the party, survived a fey drink, and taken an arrow to the backside on his first day.

Evo
“Nobody’s dead. That’s a positive.”
Izabella
“Not yet. But I am half dead.”
