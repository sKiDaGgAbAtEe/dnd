Cold Open

The grate comes off in Sekhmet’s hands. Not with tools, not with a crowbar — with claws, with leverage, with a strength roll of 19 that impresses everyone including him. The iron tears free of the stone like it was always going to, and the sewer opens beneath them: dark water, goblin torchlight, the particular smell of somewhere nothing good has been living.

Izabella flies in first. She looked and rolled a 20. It looked safe to her. She did not tell anyone about the torches until she had already descended and they had already followed.

I just wanted y’all to know that I saw those before y’all did.

✶
I — The Descent

The sewer is a maze built by people who did not want to be found. Goblin-sized passages threaded through the walls, trip-wire traps strung across the corridors, torchlight placed just wrong enough to obscure what it looks like it should illuminate. The party advances in a loose column: Izabella darting ahead, Accel Signal reading the terrain, Malc Grann navigating blind in the stretches between torches.

Raven is absent — elsewhere today, missed and noted, her absence leaving a gap in the rogue-shaped part of the formation. The party compensates. Accel steps into the scout role without being asked.

He finds the first trap before it finds anyone. Seventeen feet of fishing wire, thin as an eyelash, strung at ankle height across the passage. He marks it, steps around it, turns to the group. Stop. Right there. There’s a trap right in front of you. He crouches and disables it with an 18 on sleight of hand, the rope going slack without a sound.

There is another trap eight feet further on. He finds that one too. He rolls a 23.

Accel Signal
“Izabella — I know you want to go in heads blazing. I understand. But let me get you there safely first.”

Izabella waits. Briefly. With visible effort.

The corridor opens. Evo Guidance-boons Izabella’s perception and she rolls a nat 20, confirming what Accel already suspected: the hallway ahead is clean. No further traps. Just shadows, and the sound of movement in the walls that Accel and Sekhmet alone can hear, a many-legged weight of something repositioning itself in the dark.

✶ ✶ ✶
II — The Black Widow

It comes out of the ceiling. Eight legs, a body the size of a large dog, the red hourglass unmistakable in the torchlight. The party has exactly enough time to see it before it moves.

Initiative is rolled. The VTT’s initiative tracker refuses to include Malc Grann for approximately three minutes. By the time it is resolved, everyone has been in the interface, touched things they should not have touched, and the spider has been waiting with the patient menace of something that has nowhere else to be.

Accel Signal goes first and does the same thing he always does — Hunter’s Mark applied, longbow raised, calculation made. The spider is fast. It bites into him for 7 points before he can finish the sequence. He casts Misty Step as a bonus action, teleporting 30 feet back and out of its reach, then casts Blade Ward on himself. He has taken the hit, adjusted, and repositioned without breaking stride.

Malc Grann goes next — DM calls him “God,” and he answers to it. He is at spider range. He whips it. It takes the hit. He whips it again. The second swing breaks one of its forelegs and sends the creature staggering.

Evo watches Accel take a second bite and acts immediately. Got it, Genji. I need healing. He casts Healing Word across the gap — 1d4 plus his spellcasting modifier, rolling a 4 for a maximum result. He announces this is his last deal. He means it. He tracks Accel’s HP out loud and argues about it when Accel misreports.

Izabella lines up Spellfire Flare and rolls an 11. It misses. She stares at the result for a moment. I’m gonna kill myself. Why am I even here? Just to suffer? Fable uses his reaction to give her Bardic Inspiration. She still has it when it matters.

Sekhmet goes straight at it with the great club. He rolls a 16 to hit, connects, adds his strength modifier, and brings the club down across what Illy generously calls its “little toesies.” The spider is wounded.

The spider bites Sekhmet for 7 damage, leaving him at 3 HP. The table registers this.

Accel, reinvigorated, Hunter’s Mark still active, fires his longbow for 10 points. The spider is “heavily wounded.” One hit left in it. Malc Grann steps forward, whip out, and describes the kill: the cord breaks a leg, swings around, launches through the abdomen. Indiana Jones energy. The spider falls. Malc Grann announces that the god has fully healed during the battle. Both things are true.

✶ ✶ ✶
III — The Mimic in the Barrel

Post-combat, Fable plays a low droning note on his viol — a Minor Illusion of Sound, a subsonic thrum meant to repel whatever has been moving through the water. It works. The ripples stop. Fable explains he also has another reason for playing music, but does not elaborate. Nobody asks.

There is a barrel. Izabella wants to know what is in it. Fable says do not break the barrel. The DM says she can break the barrel if she wants. Izabella breaks the barrel.

Fable
“Inside… is a goblin.”
DM
“It’s a mimic.”

The juvenile mimic — Illy calls it this, as though size is mitigating — has one combat round of life. Accel Signal puts an arrow through it for 15 damage. It dies before it can do anything alarming. Malc Grann, who could not see Izabella because of the persistent darkness, had already decided to swap his initiative to her in case she was standing too close to it. She was.

Izabella observes that this is fine. She had a pet barrel once back home. It looked exactly like this one. The party receives this information quietly.

✶ ✶ ✶
IV — The Shark and the Respite

Sekhmet is at 3 HP. He needs a potion and he needs one now. Malc Grann produces one from his inventory — he has been carrying three, a fact he mentions with the energy of someone who only just remembered. He rolls 2d4 plus 2. The result is 9. Sekhmet goes from the edge of death to 12 HP. He says dope-tastic and the session pauses briefly so everyone can sit with that word.

The party pushes deeper. Wall creatures have been reaching through the stone to take swipes at anyone who walks too close — not traps, Illy clarifies, actual creatures, something that lives in the rock. Both Accel and Sekhmet make their dexterity saves. The sewer is a maze of wrong turns, dead ends, and passages the party has already walked. Fable tracks the route. Malc Grann inspects every wall they pass and reports on its structural integrity. This is not a joke; he has advantage on wall inspection and uses it every time.

At the waterway crossing, something moves below the surface. Izabella rolls a 22 on perception. It is some kind of shark, she reports. She cannot be certain of the species. What she can be certain of is that it already has a goblin in its mouth.

Malc Grann
“I’m going to take this hand from my inventory and chuck it into the water.”

He does. The shark thrashes after it. The party crosses while it is occupied. Malc Grann had a severed hand in his inventory. Nobody comments on the severed hand. The crossing succeeds.

They find a dead-end alcove with solid walls, a single entrance, and a platform blocking the waterway. It is, by the standards of this sewer, extremely safe. Malc Grann runs along the walls sniffing them, confirms they are structurally sound, and reports: It’s all clear, you guys. It’s clear. Accel sets an Alarm spell at the entrance, triggered to send a mental ping — specifically, Izabella’s panicked oh my God — if anything crosses the threshold. Fable does not sleep. He keeps watch, instrument in hand, playing quietly.

Short rest. Everyone recovers to maximum HP. Izabella regains a spell slot. Sekhmet regains a rage charge. Evo confirms he has not yet used his Channel Divinity because he has not had the right moment for lightning damage. He agrees to try Divine Spark next time. Fable gives Heroic Inspiration to Izabella and Sekhmet before the rest ends.

Malc Grann
“You need to treat me like a blind toddler.”
✶ ✶ ✶
V — The Peephole & the Horn

Rested, the party backtracks through the maze to a door they had not yet opened. Malc Grann presses his ear to it — perception roll, 22 — and hears breathing and footsteps. He evaluates the door with the professional skepticism of someone who has looked at a great many doors. Old, creaky, structurally tough. He draws his mace. He does not try to knock it down. He pops a hole in the middle of it instead, one precise strike, creating a 5mm peephole through which he can see a torchlit room and two goblins inside.

He reports this to Accel. They agree two goblins is manageable. Fable asks Sekhmet to push a table up against the other door to cover their rear. Sekhmet does. Fable observes that the table is a masterpiece. Illy draws it and agrees.

Evo puts his eye to the peephole. He announces he would like to cast Toll the Dead.

He does not have line of sight through 5mm of wood, technically. The table logic stands. Illy allows it. Evo corrects himself mid-cast — not Word of Radiance, which only reaches 5 feet, but Toll the Dead, which reaches any creature he can see. He can see them. Both goblins fail their wisdom saves. One dies instantly, choking on necrotic energy. The other survives the second roll. Evo opens the door.

Evo opens the door on his turn and casts Toll the Dead again on the surviving goblin. It rolls a 2 on its save. The damage is also a 2. He uses his movement to close the gap.

The goblin, wounded and aware it is losing, pulls out a horn and blows it.

Fable draws his crossbow and shoots the horn-blower before it can finish the signal. The bolt goes into its head. It falls. Horn still in hand. Whether the call went out, the party does not yet know.

The session ends here — not with resolution but with a sudden wrong sound from behind Izabella. A wall coming down. Something large. Something that was not there before. The DM motions everyone into the corner room. Izabella, who has seen it, is already moving.

You guys — you don’t understand what I just saw.

She is not wrong. A level-up waits on the other side of whatever is coming.
